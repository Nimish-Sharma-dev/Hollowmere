1. The idea in one minute
Hollowmere is a retro 2D pixel survival RPG set in a small, struggling
village at the edge of a harsh wilderness. You gather, craft, build,
hunt and fight to survive, like in the classic pixel survival games.
What is different is the storyteller.

Behind the scenes, an AI Story Director watches how you play and
decides what the world does next. It decides who in the village is your
friend and who is your enemy, what secret is rotting underneath the
village, what crisis arrives and when, and how the whole thing ends.

The village and the people are always the same, so the game stays
authored, readable and fair. But the roles those people play change
from player to player. The blacksmith who becomes your best friend in
one game may arrest you in someone else's. The poisoned well that
destroys one village never exists in another, because that player's
village is dealing with a cult instead.

Two friends can finish the game, sit down together and find that they
disagree about who the villain was.

That conversation is the product.
2. What kind of game is it?
- Genre: survival RPG with a story that adapts to the player
- View: top-down 2D, pixel art, a classic 16-bit era look
- Session: a full run takes a few hours, split into in-game days.
  Each day is a short, self-contained chunk of play, so it works in
  short sessions too
- Play style: exploration, resource gathering, crafting, light
  base-building, real-time melee and ranged combat, conversations, and
  difficult choices
- Platform feel: easy to pick up on a phone, comfortable on a
  computer
3. The promise to the player
1. "The game noticed what I did." The world reacts visibly and
   explains why.
2. "My story is mine." Nobody else gets the same villain, the same
   crisis or the same ending.
3. "The people feel real." Characters remember you, hold grudges,
   tell lies and change their minds.
4. "Every choice costs something." Survival is about scarcity, so
   kindness and cruelty both have a price.
5. "I want to compare notes." The game gives you something to talk
   about afterwards.
4. A run from start to finish
Step 1: The Campfire Prelude
Before the game begins, you sit by a fire with a mysterious stranger who
asks you seven questions. They never feel like a quiz. They feel
like a late-night conversation:
- "One loaf of bread. A hungry child and a hungry soldier. Who
  eats?"
- "You find a locked chest in an empty house. What do you do?"
- "Describe, in one sentence, what you are running from." (you type
  this one yourself)
Your answers decide: - where and how you arrive (a wounded refugee at
the gate, a thief hiding in the forest, a traveler caught in a storm) -
what you carry - who is nearby when the game begins - how the village
first treats you - the story that will be built around you
Step 2: Arrival
Scene one is different for every player. You wake up already in the
middle of a situation shaped by your answers, and the village looks at
you in a way that depends on who you seem to be.
Step 3: The days
Each day has a rhythm:
- Morning: the village stirs. Rumors have spread. The world of the
  day is announced (a heavy snowfall, a market day, a blood moon).
- Daytime: you explore, gather, craft, fight, trade and talk.
  Events and people find you.
- Evening: campfire conversations, quiet moments, a ghost story
  from a stranger.
- Night: danger, secrets and clues. The things that happen at
  night are rarely the same twice.
Step 4: The turn
Around the middle of the story, something shifts. A secret surfaces, a
trusted person is revealed to have been hiding something, or the real
danger turns out to be somewhere else entirely. Which twist you get
depends on your run.
Step 5: The ending
There are many endings, and each one is shaped by who you became, who
you saved, who you abandoned and what the village turned into.
Step 6: Your Chronicle
When it ends, you receive a storybook of your run. It tells your
story in pixel-art pages, shows what different villagers said about you
(they do not agree), reveals the hidden title the game gave you, and
hints at secrets you never found. You can share it.
5. The world
The village of Hollowmere
A small settlement of farms, a forge, a mill, a chapel, a watchtower and
a meeting hall, surrounded by forest, marsh, hills and the ruins of
something older. It is cut off from the larger world and the winters are
cruel. The village is not safe, but it is not hopeless either. Whether
it survives depends largely on you.

6. The survival loop
The survival side of the game is classic and satisfying on its own:
- Needs: health, hunger, warmth and energy. The cold is a constant
  enemy.
- Gathering: chop trees, mine stone and ore, forage berries, fish,
  hunt.
- Crafting: make tools, weapons, clothing, torches, traps and
  remedies.
- Building: raise a shelter, light fires, set up a workshop,
  fortify the village.
- Seasons: the weather shifts. Winter forces hard decisions about
  food and fuel.
- Combat: simple, readable, real-time fights against wolves,
  bandits, cultists and worse.
- The village economy: you can feed the village, rob it, trade
  with it, protect it or abandon it.
Scarcity is the engine of the drama. When food is short, every kindness
feels expensive.
7. Why every run is different
This is the heart of the game. Several layers work together, and none of
them depends on pure randomness. They all depend on you.
The Story Director
The Director is the game's storyteller. It does not run the rules
(hunger, damage and crafting are fixed and fair). It decides what
story the rules play out in. It reads how you play and keeps asking:
- Is this player coasting? Then raise the tension.
- Are they about to give up? Then offer a lifeline, with a small cost.
- Do they always take the safe path? Then put them somewhere safety is
  not an option.
- Do they always help? Then show them what helping costs.
The Director never only mirrors you. It also challenges you and
surprises you. A player who is always ruthless might meet the one
situation where mercy is rewarded. Otherwise the game would just flatter
you.
Shuffled roles
The cast is fixed, but their roles are not. In one run the miller is a
victim. In another he is the one who poisoned the well. The same lines
of dialogue, the same places, the same faces, a different truth
underneath.
A different crisis
Every run has a hidden problem at the root of the village's troubles. It
might be a poisoned well, a cult in the chapel, a hoarding mayor, a
curse tied to the old mill, or an army that is coming. The crisis shapes
everything from the rumors to the night-time events.
Secrets and clues
Each run hides several secrets. Clues are scattered for you to find, and
you will never find all of them. Whatever you do not discover, another
player might.
Relationships between the villagers
The villagers do not only care about you. They love, fear, owe and
resent each other. Your actions ripple through those relationships, so
helping one person may anger another.
A world with a memory
Villagers remember what you do. The blacksmith might raise his prices
because you stole from him, or give you a discount because you saved his
daughter. They talk to each other too, so word spreads, and it changes
as it spreads.
Rumors and gossip
Rumors are the village's nervous system. A story about you gets
exaggerated, twisted, misattributed or turned into something you never
did. "He killed two bandits" becomes "He killed ten." Rumors also
point toward real secrets, so listening carefully pays off.
A hidden title for you
Secretly, the game decides what kind of person you are. It watches and
quietly labels you:

  Title                Who you are
  The Lone Wolf    Self-reliant, distrustful, always moving
The Peacemaker   You settle disputes and avoid violence
The Scavenger    You survive on whatever you can take
The Tyrant       You rule through fear and force
The Shepherd     You protect and carry others
The Trickster    You lie, bargain and slip away

The world quietly adapts to your title. Tyrants face rebellion.
Peacemakers get dragged into feuds. Scavengers get tempted by cursed
loot. The title is revealed only at the end.
Ghost legends
When someone else finishes the game, their story becomes a legend in the
world. In your game you might find a grave, a statue or a song about "a
wanderer who burned the mill to save the village." You are playing in a
world that other players have already passed through.
The world of the day
Each day, a world-wide condition applies to everyone: eternal winter, a
plague rumor, a traveling circus, a blood moon. It gives players a
reason to come back tomorrow, and a shared topic to talk about.
8. Two players, one village
Asha and Ravi both start Hollowmere on the same day.
Asha
At the campfire: she shares the bread with the child. She would
never open a locked chest. She types: "I'm running from a fire that
took my home."

Her game: - She arrives as a wounded refugee at the village gate. -
Maren the blacksmith takes her in. They become close friends. - The
village's hidden problem is a poisoned well. - Mayor Alder is
secretly the traitor. He has been hoarding clean water and selling it. -
Pip is the only villager who has seen the truth, and nobody is
listening. - She spends the winter rationing food and rebuilding
trust. - The Director keeps testing her kindness: the village cannot
afford it all, and she has to choose who to save.

Her ending: the village survives, scarred. She is named The
Shepherd. Her Chronicle tells her that Captain Voss was hiding a
secret she never found.
Ravi
At the campfire: he takes the loaf for himself. He cracks the chest
open. He types: "I'm running from a debt."

His game: - He arrives in the forest with a stolen satchel and is
caught by Maren, who becomes his rival. - The village's hidden
problem is a cult in the chapel. - Sister Edda is the secret
leader. - Mayor Alder is only a tired leader who is genuinely trying
to hold things together. - Bram the peddler offers Ravi a dangerous
deal. - The Director tests his greed: the cult offers him power, and the
village offers him a place.

His ending: the village empties and he is left standing in the
ruins. He is named The Scavenger. Villagers speak of him very
differently.
When they compare notes
Asha: "Maren was my best friend. The mayor was the villain."
Ravi: "Maren tried to arrest me! And the mayor? He was fine. It
was Sister Edda." Asha: "Sister Edda healed me!"

Same village. Same people. Two completely different games.

10. The village as a living character
The village has moods that change with your choices: Hope, Fear,
Hunger, Trust in you, and Unity. You do not see these as menus. You
see lit windows at night, empty streets, a festival in the square or a
boarded-up chapel. When those moods reach a tipping point, big things
happen:
- Hope high: a festival, new arrivals, a golden age
- Fear high: curfews, informants, a riot
- Hunger high: theft, famine, desperate choices
- Trust high: villagers offer help, secrets and gifts
- Unity low: the village splits into factions
- Everything low: the village falls or empties
11. Endings
Endings come in families, and each family has many variations depending
on who survived and what you did:
- A golden age: the village thrives
- Scarred survival: it endures, but it is changed
- Exodus: the village empties and everyone leaves
- Collapse: the village falls
- Ruler of the ruins: you take control of what is left
- A lonely road: you walk away alone
- Something else: the village becomes something strange (a cult, a
  legend, a haunting)
- Legend: your story passes into the world for others to find
12. Tone and look
- Art: crisp, warm, readable pixel art. Cozy colors by day, deep
  blues and ember-oranges by night. Snow, rain, fire and fog are all
  part of the mood.
- Music: gentle chip-style melodies that change with the village's
  mood. Hopeful when it thrives, thin and wary when it starves.
- Writing: short, specific, human. Villagers say small, strange,
  concrete things. They do not give speeches.
- Tone: mostly grounded and a little dark. There is room for
  warmth, humor and mystery. Never grim for the sake of it.
13. Design pillars
1. Same world, different truth. The setting is stable. The story
   underneath it is not.
2. The world must explain itself. Whenever the game reacts to you,
   it shows you why.
3. Surprise over flattery. The game should challenge you, not only
   reflect you.
4. Small pieces, endless combinations. Many modular scenes,
   characters and secrets combined in unexpected ways beat a few long
   stories.
5. Fair rules, flexible story. Survival rules never cheat. Only the
   story adapts.
6. Always conversation-worthy. Every run should leave you with
   something to say to a friend.
14. What it is not
- Not an infinite random sandbox. The village, the cast and the world
  are authored and consistent.
- Not a chatbot you talk to freely. You play a game with a
  storyteller, and the storyteller works behind the scenes.
- Not a copy of any existing game. It takes inspiration from the pixel
  survival genre and builds its own world, characters and art.
15. Who it is for
- Fans of cozy-but-tense pixel survival games
- Players who love story-rich RPGs with choices that matter
- Players who like to compare outcomes and share stories
- People who enjoy replaying a game and finding something new
16. Roadmap (in plain terms)
1. The first village. One village, eight characters, a handful of
   crises, a full run from campfire to Chronicle. The goal is to prove
   that two players really end up in different games.
2. The full launch. More characters, more crises, more events, more
   endings, richer rumors, a bigger world of ghost legends, and polish.
3. Beyond. New villages and regions, seasonal events, community
   challenges and shared world stories.
17. FAQ
Is it multiplayer? Not in real time. But players share a world
through ghost legends, the daily world condition and shareable
Chronicles.

Will I see the same story twice? Very unlikely. Roles, crises,
secrets, relationships and endings are all combined differently every
run, and your own choices keep reshaping them.

Can the storyteller break the rules? No. Survival rules, combat and
crafting are fixed and fair. The storyteller decides which events happen
and who is involved, not whether you take damage.

What if I want to see everything? You cannot, in one run. That is
the point. Your Chronicle will tell you what you missed.

Can I play offline? The game always has a fallback storyteller that
keeps it playable, so the experience degrades gracefully instead of
breaking. #
